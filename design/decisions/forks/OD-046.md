## The owner's answer

One answer for the largest order: 100 lines is the largest order `PlaceOrder` of the example supports, and the bound its declaration carries. It is the example's promise and no platform limit: a command's declaration carries its own bound, chosen by its author under the adapter's stream and byte ceilings. One document per stream stays the default mapping, with the derived mapping for state that outgrows its budget. The Specs stop calling 100 provisional.

The speed targets are set here, before the benchmark, as the doc requires. They are targets for the pinned local backend on the machine of the run, and a hosted deployment is another question. The main thread had seen three single local runs (51.6, 39.1 and 184.4 ms at 1, 10 and 100 lines) when it set them, and the record says so.

- Latency without contention, the execution time of the top-level mutation as the backend's log reports it, over at least 30 accepted commands per size after 5 discarded: median at most 100 ms at 1 line, 100 ms at 10 lines and 400 ms at 100 lines; 95th percentile at most 200, 200 and 800 ms.
- Throughput without contention: 8 callers at once, each placing ten-line orders on stock no other caller touches, complete at least 20 orders a second over at least 10 seconds, with the local backend's write cap of 4 MiB a second stated beside the number.
- Contention, N commands at once on one stock item with stock for one: at N of 2 and 8 every command reaches a business outcome, one applied and the rest rejected for short stock, and the engine fails none after its bounded retries. At N of 32 the same count is reported, and a command the engine failed is a recorded cost and no failure of the experiment, because the caller repeats it under the same request key.
- A target that is missed is reported as not held with its number. No target is changed after the run.
